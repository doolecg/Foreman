Orchestration graph v2: tickets at the centre

The graph now draws the Model Engine as it really runs: the Router at the top, one worker per ticket in a dependency map, and the helper roles round the edge. One colour means "working now", and only one thing on screen ever shouts.

Read this first

  1. Everything below is a static mockup. All names, numbers and costs are sample data.
  2. Section 1 is the full graph. Section 2 is the narrow panel and pop-out. Section 3 is one ticket opened up.
  3. Section 5 has the three decisions to approve or reject. Nothing gets built until you answer them.
  4. The button below swaps the whole page between dark and light.

5. Why this look, and three decisions

Today's graph draws a fixed pipeline of roles, each with its own colour (green, purple, amber, teal, pink), in small capital letters, with marching borders on anything running. That worked with one builder. With several workers and a dependency map it becomes a rainbow, and the eye can't find the one thing that matters. This proposal keeps what works: the card shape, the Figtree type, the theme tokens, the context bar, the size switch and the pop-out. It changes three things. You can approve or reject each one on its own.

  1. One colour for "working now". The five role colours go. Foreman's own orange marks only what is active right now: a running card's edge and the lines that carry work. Amber means blocked or a watchdog alert, red means failed. Everything else, including Done, is plain ink or grey. You tell roles apart by where they sit and by their names. If you reject this, the role colours stay but become a small dot only, not borders.
  2. Tickets are the graph. The map is the tickets' dependency order, left to right, ending at the Merger. The Router sits at the top and the helper roles sit round the edge. Done tickets fold to one line so the running work gets the room. If you reject this, the graph keeps today's role pipeline and tickets go in a list under it.
  3. Only "Needs you" is loud. A decision you must make is the only filled bar, with the only filled button, above the map. Watchdog alerts are short amber text on the worker. Marching borders become a still orange edge, and only the context bar breathes slowly (none with reduced motion). Capital letter labels become sentence case. If you reject this, alerts get their own banner too and the marching edge stays.

Notes for the builder

  • Data. OrchestrationGraph needs, per ticket: the TicketRow fields from shared/tickets.ts, plus checks passed and total, context percent, tokens, cost, current file and watchdog alert. Per role: state and calls. Run totals: tokens, cost, workers busy and the cap. Memory: reads and saves for Hindsight, CodeGraph and memory.md, each null when not installed. Never fake a number: a missing one shows as a dash.
  • Layout. Column = dependency depth (a ticket with no dependencies is column 0). Inside a column, order is needs you, running, review, blocked, ready, draft, done. The Merger is always the last column. Lines are drawn after layout, from the right edge of a ticket to the left edge of the one that waits on it.
  • Sizes. Nothing under 12px. Numbers use tabular figures. Text is 13 to 14px on cards, 16px for the Router name.
  • Keyboard. Tab moves into the graph once, then arrow keys move between tickets (left and right follow dependencies, up and down move within a column). Enter opens the ticket, Esc closes it and returns focus. The "Needs you" buttons come first in tab order.
  • Screen readers. Each card is a group with a full sentence label (as in this page's markup). The lines are hidden from readers; the same facts are in the labels ("blocked by T-103"). Status changes go to a polite live region, one sentence each, at most one every 5 seconds. Only "Needs you" uses an alert.
  • Light and dark. Same token names; light swaps in a darker orange so text passes WCAG AA. Check both before calling it done.

Sample data only. Ticket names, costs and timings are made up to show a full board.
